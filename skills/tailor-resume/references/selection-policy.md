# Resume selection policy

## Summary

Keep the preferred professional identity unless the user explicitly changes it in canonical data. Tailor the focus, not the facts. Mention the strongest relevant combination of domain, stack, product context, and career progression.

## Experience

- Keep the most relevant recent role detailed.
- Include older roles individually only when they support a target requirement; otherwise group them accurately.
- Preserve overlapping freelance or part-time dates without implying full-time exclusivity.
- Use supported metrics exactly, including qualifiers such as “approximately.”
- Do not turn responsibility into ownership or achievement without evidence.

## Skills

- Put professionally supported mandatory skills first.
- Place project or study skills in a separately labeled section.
- Omit unsupported keywords entirely from the resume and list them as gaps in the handoff.
- Do not present a repository dependency as skill proficiency.
- Do not describe historical WordPress or similar experience as current proficiency when confidence is intentionally unclassified.

## Projects

- Include projects only when they add evidence not already clear from employment.
- State `professional`, `personal project`, `study project`, or `AI-assisted project` when omission would mislead.
- Distinguish product direction, testing, manual code contribution, and generated implementation.
- Prefer two or three relevant projects over a broad catalogue.

## Education and preferences

- Never describe incomplete study as a completed degree.
- Include education when required, relevant, or needed by the market; otherwise it may be concise.
- Include relocation, travel, compensation, or contract preferences only when useful for the target application.

## Privacy

Use contact details appropriate to the intended recipient. Exclude evidence notes, private source paths, reference contacts, compensation guidance, and application history.
