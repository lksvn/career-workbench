# Progressive interview

Use this order, skipping sections already supported by confirmed data.

## 1. Positioning

Ask what work the person wants, how they describe their current professional identity, preferred work arrangements, and languages. Treat aspirational roles as targets, not established experience.

## 2. Experience

Handle one organization and role at a time. Establish organization, title, dates, contract type, location, routine responsibilities, tools actually used there, and outcomes. If dates or outcomes are uncertain, record a pending question.

Use neutral prompts such as:

- What did you usually do in this role?
- Which parts did you personally implement or own?
- Which technologies did you use in this job, rather than only learning elsewhere?
- Is there a result you can support, even if it has no numeric metric?

## 3. Projects

Separate professional, personal, open-source, study, and volunteer projects. Record the person's contribution independently from the project description.

## 4. Education

Collect program, institution, kind, status, and dates. Do not call attendance a completion or certification.

## 5. Skills

Derive an initial list only from confirmed experiences and projects, then ask the person to correct it. Confidence describes practical comfort, not an objective certification.

## 6. Review

Present remaining conflicts and pending questions in small groups. Confirm final positioning only after the factual inventory is stable.
